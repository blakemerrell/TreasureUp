## What it's for

Blake: "Let's re-do that temple. I'm not in love with it. The tower seems
weird." The tower came from 2 Chronicles 3:4, where the porch's height is
"an hundred and twenty" cubits (180 ft, four times the house). 1 Kings 6
gives the porch no height, and some ancient copies read twenty, so most
drawings of Solomon's temple have no tower at all. Shown the three drawn to
one scale (a 120-cubit porch, a 20-cubit porch, and Herod's temple of
Jesus' day), Blake chose Solomon's with a 20-cubit porch: truest to "after
the manner of the temple of Solomon" (2 Nephi 5:16), since the Nephites
never saw Herod's.

## The picture

- **`temple.png`**, 4 × 4, the game's isometric angle, light from the upper
  left: the long house of pale hewn stone, three times as long as it is
  wide and the tallest part, carved with cherubim, palms and open flowers
  picked out in gold, a gold cornice and a row of narrow high windows
  (1 Kings 6:2, 4, 29); lower side chambers along it (6:5); at its front a
  porch lower than the house, its gold doors open on warm light (6:3, 32);
  before the porch two great pillars of brass standing free, with capitals of
  lily-work (7:15–22); in the white court the great golden lampstand with
  seven lit lamps (game license, as in 021), the altar of brass with its
  steps and the sea on twelve oxen.
- Still the tallest building on the field (about 705 px above its front
  corner at 150 px a square, the Rameumptom 480, the palace 430).
