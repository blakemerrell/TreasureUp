📜 Slide 2 · The Restoration Discovery

Joseph Smith’s Translation Flips It Around

When Joseph Smith read Isaiah 65:1 by revelation, the Lord restored what the prophet actually meant:

✨ Joseph Smith Translation · Isaiah 65:1
“I am found of them who seek after me. I give unto all them that ask of me; I am not found of them that sought me not…”

Why it matters: God never plays hide-and-seek with His children. Every time you open the scriptures or kneel to pray, you are seeking Him—and He promises you will find Him.

Why Swipeable Decks Beat Flat Text Sheets

One Idea Per Slide · Zero Redundancy

Previously, tapping an Insight or Deep Dive card opened a plain bottom sheet with a paragraph of text and a collapsed <details>🤿 Deep dive</details> accordion—or in the old creamPlayerHtml, showed generic boilerplate text on Slides 2 and 3.

Now every Deep Dive, Scholar Insight, Hebrew Word, and Come Follow Me lesson uses a real 3–4 slide Story Deck:

  1. Slide 1 (The Hook & Verse): Shows the exact scripture verse with an instant Plain / KJV toggle and frames the question or contrast.
  2. Slide 2 (The Real Discovery): Unpacks the actual scholar insight, Hebrew root, or prophet’s story—never generic filler.
  3. Slide 3 (Media & Outside Links): Plays the exact timestamped video clip (e.g., followHIM or Latter Day Kids) and gives clean 1-tap links to Gospel Library ↗ or Scripture Central ↗ when you want to read the full article.
  4. Slide 4 (1-Tap Takeaway + XP): Lets you or Javan tap a takeaway chip or jot a quick note and claim the XP.
❌ Before · Why Scripture Notes Felt Redundant

Says the Exact Same Thing 3 Times in a Row

In content/weeks.js + openInsNote(), every note stored 3 nearly identical strings (glance, text, and short) plus a duplicate plain.notes entry—and printed all of them when tapped:

8 Then shall thy light break forth as the morning… the glory of the LORD shall be thy rereward.

📝 Plain note (#1): “Rereward” means the rear guard at the back of an army.
📘 Margin button (#2 - glance): “Rearward” is an old word for rear guard: God’s glory guards from behind, so no one is left behind. ›
AND WHEN YOU TAPPED THAT MARGIN BUTTON:

Isaiah 58:8 [Prints the whole verse a 2nd time]

In a sentence (#3 - short): Soldiers at the back of an army protect it from behind. God’s glory is like that, gathering up anyone left behind.

Body (#4 - text): Isaiah 58:8 promises, “the glory of the LORD shall be thy rearward.” The Old Testament Student Manual, which spells it rereward, explains that it’s an old word for “rear guard”: the soldiers who protect the back of an army on the march…

✅ After · Crisp, Single-Voice Inline Study Cards

One Compact Pill → Expands In-Place with Zero Repetition

We deduplicate plain.notes and margin notes on the same verse, ditch the repeated “In a sentence” + repeated verse text, and turn the note into a crisp, expandable visual card right under the verse:

8 Then shall thy light break forth as the morning, and thine health shall spring forth speedily: and thy righteousness shall go before thee; the glory of the LORD shall be thy rereward.

📘 Old Testament Student Manual Isaiah 58:8

In King James English, rereward means the rear guard—the elite soldiers who march at the very back of a column to protect stragglers and make sure no one is ambushed or left behind.

💡 The Promise: Your righteous choices lead out in front, and the Lord’s glory closes in behind you—having your back even when you stumble.
